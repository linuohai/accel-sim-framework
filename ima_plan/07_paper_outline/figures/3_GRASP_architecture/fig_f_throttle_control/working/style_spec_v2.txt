### Review & Enhancements

1. **Color Accessibility & Line Styles**: While Matplotlib's default blue (`#1f77b4`) and red (`#d62728`) are distinct in color, they can map to similar luminance values in grayscale print. 
   * *Enhancement*: The current use of solid vs. dashed lines is good. We will further differentiate by making the Threshold line slightly thicker (`lw=2.0`) and using a distinct dotted style for the baseline. 
2. **Font Consistency**: Academic papers require serif fonts (Times New Roman). 
   * *Enhancement*: Explicitly specify `rcParams['font.family'] = 'serif'` and enforce a minimum of 8pt for annotations and 9pt for axis labels.
3. **Data-Ink Ratio**: The mapping box on the right is useful but adds chart junk if heavily bordered. 
   * *Enhancement*: Remove the border of the mapping box. Use direct labeling for the reference lines rather than adding them to a legend.
4. **Annotation Clarity**: The legend is currently doing too much work. 
   * *Enhancement*: Direct label the "MSHR Occupancy" and "Effective Threshold" lines right at their endpoints in the right margin. This drastically reduces the cognitive load (the "< 5 seconds" rule).
5. **Axis Formatting**: Raw cycle counts (e.g., 10000) are hard to read.
   * *Enhancement*: Apply a custom formatter to the X-axis to display `10K`, `20K`, etc. Ensure Y-axes are strictly bounded (e.g., `0` to `100`).
6. **Cooldown Shading**: Throttling is a system-wide state. 
   * *Enhancement*: The `#ff9896` shading must span **both** the top and bottom charts to visually link the timeline across both subplots.
7. **Causal Arrows**: Standard annotations won't cross subplots easily. 
   * *Enhancement*: Use `matplotlib.patches.ConnectionPatch` to draw arrows directly from the bottom chart's accuracy drop up to the top chart's threshold drop.
8. **Legend Optimization**: 
   * *Enhancement*: With direct labeling, the legend can be reduced to just two items: "Without Throttle Baseline" and "Cooldown Region (DATA_PF Suppressed)".
